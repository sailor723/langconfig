What we add

One slot on the candidate that holds a number

:yearsExperience  a  owl:DatatypeProperty ;
    rdfs:domain :Candidate ;
    rdfs:range  xsd:integer .

In plain words

Now we can say “this candidate has 5 years of experience”. The slot belongs to candidates, and it only accepts a whole number.

Why this is next

It is the simplest thing you can say about a person, and it is the one every job ad asks for. It is also a number, not a thing — which is why it lives in a different place in the tool than a skill will (step 4).

What the reader sees

Still one box, but now it has a slot on it: yearsExperience.

Optional technical note

A data property, holding a literal value like 5. rdfs:domain says who may have it, rdfs:range says what may go in it.

“About which class” and “which panel” are two different questions

The confusion worth avoiding

“Which class is this property about?” and “which panel does it show up in?” have different answers, and both properties below are about a candidate:

PropertyPanelValue type
:yearsExperienceData propertiesa number 5
:hasSkillObject propertiesanother entity :Python

The value's type decides the panel. The class the property is about is a separate thing, and it is the same for both.

Why not the skill link yet?

One thing per step

The obvious second property is “has skill”. But a skill link points at a box, and that box does not exist until step 3. Adding both at once would leave the reader unable to tell what each one did.
